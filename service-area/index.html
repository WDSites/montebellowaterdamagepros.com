Home base: Montebello, CA

Montebello covers about 8.37 square miles in Los Angeles County, roughly 8 miles east of downtown Los Angeles. Its ZIP code is 90640. The city borders Monterey Park, South San Gabriel and Rosemead to the north, Commerce to the south, Pico Rivera to the east and Los Angeles and East Los Angeles to the west.

The Rio Hondo runs along the east side, and main roads include Whittier Boulevard and San Gabriel Boulevard. The full set of services we dispatch is on the services page, starting with water mitigation and emergency water removal.

Because Montebello touches four neighbors and the river, a single storm or outage can affect more than one city at once. Knowing the area your address falls in helps the dispatcher plan the order of calls and avoid sending a crew to the wrong side of a boundary.

What changes from city to city

A burst pipe is a burst pipe anywhere, but the details around it change. In Monterey Park, hillside lots and busy corridors like Atlantic Boulevard shape how water travels and how crews reach a building. In Pico Rivera, the Rio Hondo and the San Gabriel River, plus the nearby dam, make flood coverage a real question. In Rosemead, apartments and many water companies make renter documentation and the water bill the first things to check.

Those differences are why each city has its own page instead of a copied template. Read yours before you need it, and keep the page open on your phone if you have already had a leak.

Before you need us

Three small tasks make an emergency easier. Find the main water shut-off and test that it turns. Note which company supplies your water and keep its number in your phone. Save the 24/7 line, (213) 776-0579, as a contact so it is one tap away at 3 a.m.

Renters can add a fourth task: ask the landlord where the building shut-off is and how to report a leak after hours. Owners can add a call to their insurer to ask whether sewer backup and flood coverage are part of the policy. All of it takes under an hour and saves time when water is moving.

Questions people ask about coverage

People often ask whether the crew can reach a neighboring city on the same night. The answer depends on how many calls are open and how far apart they are. That is why same-day response is offered when crews are available and an arrival time is not promised.

Another common question is whether a loss has to be large enough to call. It does not. A wet wall, a damp carpet or a stain that grew overnight is worth a call, because moisture readings answer the question quickly.

A third question comes from people who live between two cities, such as near the Montebello and Monterey Park line or close to the Rio Hondo. Give the street address when you call and the dispatcher will confirm the area on the spot.

Water suppliers across the area

Water service varies by city, and it matters in an emergency. Montebello lists four providers, Pico Rivera is served by its city water division and the Pico Water District, Monterey Park runs its own city water utility, and Rosemead is served by six companies. Check your bill so you know who to call about a leak before your meter.

Whatever the source, the first step inside is the same: shut off the main valve, keep people clear of contaminated water and call the 24/7 line.
