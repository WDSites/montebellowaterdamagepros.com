Where La Merced is

La Merced comes from Rancho La Merced, a land grant that Mexican governor Manuel Micheltorena gave to Casilda Soto de Lobo in 1844. The Juan Matias Sanchez Adobe, built in 1845, stands at 946 North Adobe Avenue and has belonged to the City of Montebello since 1972, according to the National Park Service and a historical marker. Wikipedia places the adobe in what it calls East Montebello.

An environmental group's 2012 article describes a La Merced neighborhood next to the Montebello Hills, the open oil field in north-central Montebello. We cannot give exact boundaries. If you are unsure whether your address counts, give the dispatcher your street and the nearest cross street.

Rain, roofs and the first signs

Rain is one way water gets into a home anywhere in the city. For Los Angeles, the National Weather Service recorded about 8.51 inches over three days in the storm of February 4 to 6, 2024, and about 4.1 inches on February 4 alone. That is a downtown Los Angeles figure and not a La Merced measurement.

A brown ring on a ceiling, bubbling paint or a damp smell after rain usually points to a roof, a flashing or a gutter. Photograph it and read roof leak water damage. If water ran down a wall, storm water damage explains what is different about rain that came in from outside.

Street flooding is not the same as water in the house

One public stormwater study lists Lincoln Avenue, between Avenida De La Merced and San Gabriel Boulevard, among known flooding spots. The study was prepared for a City of Montebello project under Safe Clean Water Los Angeles. It describes a street, not a house.

If water crosses a threshold and enters your house, it becomes a building problem. Keep out of water that may be electrified or contaminated, photograph what you can from a dry spot and call. Outside flood water is covered on the flood damage restoration page.

Safety steps while you wait

Do not wade into dark water

If the water covers outlets or came from outside, stay out. It may carry contamination or be electrically live.

Close what you can reach

Shut the main valve if a pipe broke. For a roof leak, move belongings from under the drip and place a bucket.

Call the right party

Call the utility or 911 for downed lines, sparks or a gas smell. Call the number on this page for the water.

Protect what is dry

Lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks. Move rugs, books and electronics to a dry room.

How the crew removes and dries

The crew pumps or extracts standing water, then reads moisture in walls, ceilings and floors with moisture meters. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry what can be saved. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard crews use as a reference for this work.

Wet insulation and soaked drywall near the floor are often removed because they do not dry reliably in place. After drying, water damage repair rebuilds what was removed.

Insurance questions after rain

A roof leak or a burst pipe is often treated differently from rising water. Ask your insurer which applies to your policy before you sign a scope. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here, so check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Photos, readings and receipts help describe the damage. They do not decide coverage.

Around La Merced

The same service area reaches Pico Rivera across the Rio Hondo and Rosemead to the north. For the whole city, see water damage restoration in Montebello.

Open hills next door and what to watch

The Montebello Hills are about 487 acres east of Montebello Boulevard, according to PBS SoCal's history page, with oil wells that still operate. They are open land, and the city's long-running plans for homes there remain a public debate.

For a nearby homeowner the practical point is drainage. Keep gutters and yard drains clear before rain, and watch where water leaves your lot. A drain that sends water against the foundation can become a leak inside.
