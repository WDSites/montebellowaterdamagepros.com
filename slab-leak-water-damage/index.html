Clues that water is leaking under your floor

A slab leak rarely announces itself with a puddle. The first clues are small and easy to blame on something else. Carpet feels damp along one wall, a tile floor has a warm patch, or you hear a faint hiss when every faucet is closed.

Start with the meter. A water meter check is the simplest way to confirm a hidden leak. Read the meter, use no water for two hours, then read it again. If the reading moved, there is probably a leak somewhere on your side of it.

What a slab leak is and which homes it concerns

A slab leak is a failed pipe in or beneath a concrete foundation. Common causes include corrosion on older metal lines, a loosened fitting, a pipe rubbing on rough concrete and ground movement that strains a line. Hot water lines can wear faster because heat adds expansion and contraction.

American Community Survey data from 2023, published by the Census Bureau, puts the median year Montebello homes were built at 1963. Housing there splits into 56.6 percent single-family, 41.8 percent multi-family and 1.6 percent mobile homes. A median that old means plenty of plumbing has decades behind it, so ask your plumber about the age and material of your lines.

We cannot give you a local count of slab homes, and nobody should guess one. Your house is either on a slab or on a raised floor over a crawl space, and you probably already know which. If yours has a crawl space, the crawl space water damage page fits better.

Slab leak safety in the first hour

Go to the main water shut-off, usually at the meter box or where the line enters the house, and turn it until it stops. If water is pooling near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay clear of it.

If water has crossed the floor, leave the panel alone unless it sits in a dry spot you can reach on dry footing. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas hazard. A gas smell near a hissing sound means you leave at once and call from outside.

  • Close the main water valve
  • Stay out of water near outlets and the panel
  • Photograph wet floors and walls before moving furniture
  • Call a plumber or leak detection company to find the break

Who fixes the pipe and who dries the house

We do not repair plumbing. A plumber or leak detection company locates the break with listening gear or thermal tools and then repairs it. Reaching a pipe under concrete can mean cutting the slab, and that work belongs to the plumber. Rerouting the line through a wall or attic is another option your plumber can explain.

Ask the plumber to check water pressure while the pipes are open. The California Plumbing Code, as summarized by a city water utility, calls for a pressure regulator where pressure exceeds 80 psi. High pressure stresses hoses, valves and fittings, so it is a cheap question to ask before the next failure.

Once the leak is stopped, our crew takes over the water. If the water is coming from inside a wall or ceiling instead of the floor, the burst pipe water damage page is the better match.

How the crew dries a floor that sat over a leak

We start with a moisture survey and infrared scan to trace how far the water traveled from the break. Slab water spreads sideways through flooring, baseboards and the bottom of wall cavities, so the wet area is often larger than the stain.

Soaked carpet pad and baseboard come out first because they hold water against everything near them. Extraction units and weighted extractors pull water from carpet and hard floors. Air movers then push air across the surface while LGR dehumidifiers remove moisture from the room.

Hardwood and engineered flooring may be dried with injection drying mats that draw moisture through the boards. Concrete holds water longest, so we keep checking it and we also read the rooms next door. We work to ANSI/IICRC S500 practice. Supply line water starts as category 1 and can degrade if it sits.

What moves the price of a slab leak job

Price follows the size of the problem, and the readings decide that, not a guess over the phone. These are the factors that change a scope most often.

  • Square footage of wet flooring and subfloor edges
  • Flooring type: carpet, tile, hardwood or laminate
  • Whether water crossed into wall cavities or neighboring rooms
  • How long the leak ran before it was found
  • Number of drying visits the readings call for
  • Whether pad, baseboard or drywall must be removed

Slab leaks and your policy

Coverage varies a lot between policies. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, while a gradual leak or neglect often is not. Some policies treat the cost of reaching the pipe differently from the water damage that followed it.

A leaking pipe is not a flood, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer before work begins. Our photos and moisture readings give you records to send.

Mistakes that make a slab leak worse

Do not pull up flooring before you photograph it. The stains and the wet edges are your evidence of where water traveled. Do not lay a new pad or seal the floor to hide a damp spot either, because that traps moisture under it.

A dry floor surface does not prove a dry slab. Concrete can hold water well after carpet feels fine. Run extension cords for fans or heaters only across dry floors, and keep them out of standing water.

Slab leaks in Montebello: bills, tenants and neighbors

More than one water provider serves Montebello. Your water bill names yours. A leak before the meter is generally the utility's concern and one after it is the owner's, so the bill tells you who to call.

Census 2020 counted more renters than owners in Montebello, 55.6 percent of 20,308 units, which is why the landlord should hear about a slab leak in writing. If you rent, tell your landlord in writing the day you notice damp floors and keep copies. Photograph everything so the record shows when the damage was found.

Questions

Slab leak questions from Montebello households

Is emergency slab leak water damage cleanup available in Montebello?

Yes. Someone answers the dispatch line day and night, weekends included. Crews are sent the same day when they are available, but dispatch does not commit to an arrival time. A plumber still has to stop the leak itself.

How can I tell a slab leak from a leak inside a wall?

A moving meter, a warm floor spot or damp carpet edges point down. A stained ceiling or wet drywall points to a wall or attic line. A plumber confirms the location, and we dry whatever got wet.

Do you break the concrete to reach the pipe?

No. Opening a slab is plumbing work, so the plumber or leak detection company handles it. Our crew deals with the water, the wet flooring and the drying afterward.

How long will my floors take to dry?

It depends on the readings. Concrete holds moisture longer than framing, so drying is judged by readings on each material. Concrete and hardwood can need more time.

Can a leak under the floor lead to mold?

Mold needs moisture, so damp materials that sit are the concern. Fast drying lowers the odds but nobody can promise it never appears. If growth is already visible, see our mold remediation page.

What should a Montebello renter do about a slab leak?

Tell the landlord in writing, photograph the damage and close the main valve if you can reach it. Call us for water removal and drying. The landlord or owner arranges the plumber for the pipe.
