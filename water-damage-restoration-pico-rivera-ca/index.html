Between two rivers, below a dam

Pico Rivera sits on a plain between the Rio Hondo and the San Gabriel River, below Whittier Narrows. Whittier Narrows Dam is located in the city. In 2019 the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reported that the dam no longer meets its tolerable-risk guidelines, with reporting saying it could fail in a 900-year storm.

That is not a prediction, and it is not a reason to panic. It is a reason to look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer about flood coverage, since flood damage is usually excluded from standard policies. The practical step is to know before the water arrives.

When water is spreading in Pico Rivera

With roughly seven in ten homes owner-occupied, most Pico Rivera losses are the owner's to fix. That makes fast mitigation more valuable, because the owner carries the cost of anything that stays wet.

Call the line as soon as you see water. If the source is a pipe, shut off the main valve. If it is a flooded yard or street water entering, do not wade through it, and keep power off if the panel is wet.

An earthquake and your plumbing

A magnitude 4.4 earthquake was centered in Pico Rivera on March 16, 2010. A shake that size can loosen water heater connections and crack older supply fittings without any visible damage. After any quake, check under sinks, around the water heater and along exterior walls for dampness, because a slow leak shows up days later as a stain.
